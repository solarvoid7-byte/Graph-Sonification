Graph Sonification

Listen to a linear or quadratic graph. The page runs in your browser. There is no account and nothing to install.

How the sound works

Playback walks the graph from left to right in 3 seconds. Pitch follows the height of the line: 500 + 30 × y hertz. A point at y = 10 is 800 hertz. A point at y = −10 is 200 hertz.

  • Main pitch stays between 200 and 800 hertz. If the graph leaves y = −10 to 10, the pitch holds at that limit.
  • A short beep marks a crossing of the x-axis or the y-axis.
  • A 100 hertz hum plays while the graph is below zero.
  • Volume is locked at −6 dB (half level, about 75 dBA in this project). There is no volume slider.

Voice

Turn voice commands on, then say exactly “Play”, “Pause”, or “Reset”. The page says “Listening” when the microphone opens, and plays a short tone when it closes.

Chrome and Safari can listen. Firefox uses the keyboard. A page opened as a file may block the microphone; keyboard playback still works.

What to listen for

  • y = 2x + 3 rises.
  • y = −x + 2 falls.
  • y = 0.5x rises more slowly and beeps at the origin.
  • y = x² − 4 falls, then rises.
  • y = −0.5x² + 5 rises, then falls. The hum appears when it goes below zero.
  • y = 2x² + 1 climbs quickly away from the middle.

Ear safety

If a value is outside the allowed limits, playback does not start and a 400 hertz tone plays. The axis beep is 50 milliseconds at 800 hertz, the top of the safe range. The plan mentioned 1000 hertz; that pitch is above the success-criteria limit, so it is not used.
